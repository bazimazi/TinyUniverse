# Design research

Research reviewed on 2026-10-02. These are design inferences from official product descriptions, not measured retention claims. No third-party assets or code are copied.

- [Kittens Game](https://kittensgame.com/web/) and its [offline progression documentation](https://wiki.kittensgame.com/s/en/game-tabs/time): incremental complexity works well with explicit offline caps and visible rewards. Tiny Universe begins with four resources and reveals new systems through milestones.
- [WorldBox](https://www.superworldbox.com/): observing autonomous civilizations and changing their environment supports the indirect god-game fantasy. Give worlds distinct histories and interventions with consequences.
- [Stellaris](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/stellaris/about): exploration, anomalies, procedural species and diplomacy provide complementary long-term loops. Use bounded exploration jobs, trait-driven actors and branching discoveries.
- [Universe Sandbox](https://www.universesandbox.com/): celestial interactions and changing habitability make physical systems understandable through their consequences. Use normalized Keplerian orbits rather than astronomical precision or expensive N-body physics.
- [MDN canvas optimization](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Canvas_API/Tutorial/Optimizing_canvas): bound pixel density and draw counts, separate simulation from animation and cache repeated scenery.
- [Vite getting started](https://vite.dev/guide/) and [Node TypeScript support](https://nodejs.org/api/typescript.html): a static browser build and native headless TypeScript tests keep the prototype portable and reproducible.

UX decisions: large touch targets, portrait layout, visible next objective, contextual panels, reduced motion, save export and no paid progression gates. Anonymous analytics and monetization remain replaceable future services; no network telemetry is collected.
